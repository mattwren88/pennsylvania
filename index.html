Keystone Atlas

A layered map of Pennsylvania

Every Pennsylvania historical marker with a known location — 2,585 of them, merged from the PHMC's export and its live map service — plus forty-odd layers of parks, trails, waterways, boundaries, abandoned mine lands, natural landmarks and vanished places, drawn over real street geography.

How to use it

  • Search everything — markers, every loaded layer and the USGS gazetteer at once.
  • Layers load the first time you tick them; large ones fetch only what the viewport covers.
  • Filter markers by county, type, and dedication decade.
  • Copy link captures the view, base map, active layers and decade in a shareable URL.

Data sources & credits

Live layers come from PASDA (Pennsylvania Spatial Data Access, Penn State), whose underlying agencies include the PHMC, DCNR, PennDOT, DEP, the Fish & Boat and Game Commissions, WeConservePA, FEMA and NGS. County geometry is U.S. Census via us-atlas. Street tiles © OpenStreetMap contributors; hillshade and satellite imagery by Esri; USGS topographic quads via Esri; railway overlay © OpenRailwayMap contributors (CC-BY-SA). Marker text and details are from the Pennsylvania Historical & Museum Commission.

Honesty about the data

  • County high points are the highest named summit per county; the true maximum is often an unnamed rise.
  • Covered bridges and fire towers are filtered from the GNIS gazetteer by name — named features, not a complete inventory.
  • "Places that are gone" are features GNIS marks (historical); no closure date or cause is recorded.
  • Trail activity flags are undocumented codes read as permitted/not; unknowns are omitted.
  • A fetch that hits the paging cap says so rather than reporting a truncated count.
